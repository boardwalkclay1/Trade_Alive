Stage 8 – Charts & Candles

What a Chart Really Is

A chart is a visual record of how price has moved over time. It is not a prediction device; it is a history book written in real time. Every bar or candle is the result of buyers and sellers interacting in the market.

When you look at a chart, you are seeing where buyers were willing to step in, where sellers pushed back, and where the balance of power shifted. It is a map of the auction you learned about in Stage 7.

Your job is not to worship the chart. Your job is to read it calmly and connect it back to simple questions: “Who is winning right now? Up or down from here? Does it match my plan?”

Red and Green Candles

Most trading platforms show price using candles. A green candle usually means price closed higher than it opened in that period. A red candle usually means price closed lower than it opened.

The “body” of the candle shows the open and close. The thin lines above or below (wicks) show the extremes—how high or low price moved during that period.

A series of strong green candles tells a story of aggressive buyers. A series of strong red candles tells a story of aggressive sellers. Mixed, choppy candles tell a story of indecision or balance.

Connecting Charts Back to Options

When you choose a call or a put, you are making a decision about direction from your entry. The chart helps you see whether buyers or sellers have been winning recently, and where that might change.

A disciplined trader does not guess. They look at the chart, read the story, and then decide whether taking a position makes sense inside their rules. The chart is not a guarantee. It is context.

Later chapters will go deeper into patterns and indicators, but it all starts here: red and green candles telling you which side has had control.
