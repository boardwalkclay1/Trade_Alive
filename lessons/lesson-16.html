Stage 16 – Moving Averages & Trend

Short, Medium, and Long Moving Averages

Moving averages can be set to different lengths. Shorter ones (like 9 or 10) react quickly to price. Medium ones (like 20 or 21) balance speed and stability. Longer ones (like 50 or 200) move slowly and show the bigger trend.

I often use a combination, because it lets me see the “tempo” of price: fast movement, the main swing, and the broader direction. You do not need to memorize every setting right now—just understand that each length sees the market at a different speed.

Later, you can decide which specific moving averages become part of your personal plan.

Trend and Dynamic Support/Resistance

When price respects a moving average—bouncing near it multiple times—it often acts like dynamic support in an uptrend or dynamic resistance in a downtrend.

In an uptrend, you may see price pull back toward a rising moving average, then buyers step in again. In a downtrend, you may see price pop up into a falling moving average, then sellers push it back down.

This behavior gives structure to your ideas. Instead of random entries, you can look for trades that align with the trend and respect these dynamic levels.

Discipline: Not Worshipping the Line

It is tempting to treat a moving average as a magic line: “If price touches it, I must buy or sell.” That is not how this academy works.

Moving averages are part of a bigger system. You still need to consider the auction context, candles, patterns, timeframes, and your risk and exit rules.

Used with discipline, moving averages help you avoid random trades. Used without discipline, they become just another way to justify emotional decisions.
