Stage 3 – Calls, Puts, and Direction

Connecting Up/Down to Calls and Puts

In Stage 1, you learned the simplest model: you put money in, the market can go up or down from your entry, and then you take money out. Now we map that simple model onto options language.

A call option is a contract that generally benefits when the price of the underlying ticker moves up from where you entered. A put option is a contract that generally benefits when the price of the underlying ticker moves down from where you entered.

You are not required to know every detail of options yet. What matters here is the link: calls = up, puts = down, always anchored to your entry.

Why This Mapping Matters

Without this simple mapping, options can feel like a foreign language. Traders get lost in symbols and forget the core question: “Am I positioning for up or down from here?”

When you think in terms of calls and puts, you must always tie it back to: “Where am I entering? What direction am I expecting next? How disciplined am I about exits?” This keeps you from treating options like lottery tickets.

In future stages, when you see an options chain full of numbers and codes, this up/down, call/put framework will keep you grounded.
