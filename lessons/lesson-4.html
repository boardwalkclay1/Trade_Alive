The Options Chain: Your Contract Map

When you open an options page, you’re looking at a structured table called the options chain. This is where you choose the exact contract you want to trade. A disciplined trader reads this page slowly and intentionally — the same way a pilot reads instruments.

   KEY:
   • STRIKE = Price level of the contract
   • BID = What buyers are willing to pay
   • ASK = What sellers want to receive
   • EXPIRATION = When the contract expires
   • QUANTITY = How many contracts you choose (multiplies risk)

Every row is a strike. Every column is a piece of information. Your job is to read this calmly and choose the contract that matches your directional idea and your discipline.

Reading the Options Chain

The options chain is a table of contracts with different strike prices and expiration dates. Each row represents a strike. Each column shows information like bid, ask, last price, and sometimes volume or open interest.

You use this page to choose the exact contract you want to trade. You are not clicking randomly — you are selecting a contract that matches your expectation and your rules.

Key Elements: Strike, Expiration, Quantity, Bid, Ask

Strike price: The price level associated with the contract. It anchors your call or put.

Expiration date: The last date the contract is valid. Options lose value faster as expiration approaches.

Quantity: How many contracts you trade. One contract typically controls 100 shares. Quantity multiplies both risk and reward.

Bid: What buyers are willing to pay.
Ask: What sellers want to receive.
The difference is the spread.

Impatient traders overpay. Disciplined traders use limit orders and wait for good pricing.
