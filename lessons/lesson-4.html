Stage 4 – Options Page Breakdown

Reading the Options Chain

When you open an options page on a broker platform, you see an options chain: a table of contracts with different strike prices and expiration dates.

Each row usually represents a strike price. Each column holds information like bid, ask, last price, and sometimes volume and open interest. You use this page to choose the exact contract you want to trade.

You are not just clicking randomly. A disciplined trader reads this table like a pilot reads instruments—slow, clear, and intentional.

Key Elements: Strike, Expiration, Quantity, Bid, Ask

Strike price: The price level associated with the contract. It is a reference point for your call or put.

Expiration date: The last date the contract is valid. Time matters. As expiration approaches, options lose value faster.

Quantity: How many contracts you are trading. One options contract typically controls 100 shares of the underlying ticker. Quantity multiplies both risk and potential reward.

Bid: What buyers are currently willing to pay. Ask: What sellers are currently asking to receive. The difference is the spread.

The bid and ask matter because impatience can make you overpay or accept a poor price, while disciplined limit orders can help you enter and exit more efficiently.

Interactive Chain Example

Below is a simplified options chain for a fictional ticker trading around $100. This is only for training purposes.

Strike Type Bid Ask Expires
95 Call 2.50 2.70 This Friday
100 Call 1.50 1.65 This Friday
105 Put 1.80 1.95 This Friday

Notice how each row tells you: what price level (strike), what type (call or put), what people are willing to pay (bid), what others are asking (ask), and when it expires.
