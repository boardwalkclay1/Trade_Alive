Stage 15 – Moving Averages Foundation

Why Moving Averages Matter So Much

By now, you’ve seen the market as an auction, learned basic chart reading, patterns, and how to build a structured trade idea. In this chapter, we focus on a tool that I personally value above all others: moving averages.

Over more than ten years of trading—through wins, losses, and the stretch where I turned $400 into $40,000 in five days—moving averages have been one of my most reliable guides. Not because they are magic, but because they help me see trend and structure clearly.

This chapter is about teaching you how to see what I see when I look at a chart with moving averages: the flow of buyers and sellers, the rhythm of pullbacks, and where a disciplined trader might build or exit a position.

What a Moving Average Actually Is

A moving average is simply the average price over a certain number of candles, updated as each new candle forms. Common settings include 9, 20, 50, or 200 periods.

On your chart, it shows up as a smooth line that follows price. When price is generally above a rising moving average, buyers have often been in control. When price is generally below a falling moving average, sellers have often been in control.

Think of it as a way to smooth out noise and see the “tilt” of the market. It does not predict the future. It gives you context for the present.

Connecting Moving Averages to Previous Lessons

In earlier chapters, you learned to ask: “Up or down from here? Who is winning the auction? Does this fit my plan?” Moving averages help you answer those questions with more clarity.

When you later build a mock trade and walk through the enter/exit simulator, your moving averages will act like the backbone of the chart: showing trend, helping you judge pullbacks, and supporting your calls or puts with structure instead of guesswork.

You will learn to use moving averages together with candles, patterns, and timeframes to confirm or reject trade ideas, not to force trades that aren’t there.
