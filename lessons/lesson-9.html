Stage 9 – Chart Patterns Overview

Patterns as Repeated Behavior

Chart patterns are not magic shapes. They are visual representations of repeated behavior between buyers and sellers. When similar pressure shows up over and over, price often reacts in similar ways.

Examples of Common Patterns

Flag Pattern — a strong move, then a tight drifting pause.

──────────────────────────────────────────────

Flags often appear in strong trends. They do not guarantee continuation — they simply show controlled, disciplined selling or buying pressure.

These patterns do not guarantee outcomes. They simply help you understand where buyers and sellers have drawn lines in the sand.

Patterns and Options Trades

Patterns add context to your directional ideas. A bullish pattern near support may support a call idea. A bearish pattern near resistance may support a put idea.

Patterns are part of a larger process — not the entire process. You still need discipline, direction, risk control, and an exit plan.

A disciplined trader sees a pattern, connects it to the broader story, and then decides whether a trade fits within their rules.
