Stage 9 – Chart Patterns Overview

Patterns as Repeated Behavior

Chart patterns are not magic shapes. They are visual representations of repeated behavior between buyers and sellers. When similar pressure shows up over and over, price often reacts in similar ways.

Flags, wedges, double tops, and double bottoms are just ways to describe where price pushed, paused, failed, or broke through. They give structure to the story the chart is already telling.

In this stage, you will get a light overview. Later, individual patterns will earn their own focused chapters. For now, you just need to understand what patterns are and why they matter.

Examples of Common Patterns

A flag often forms after a strong move, when price pauses and drifts in a smaller range before potentially continuing in the same direction.

A double top forms when price pushes into a similar high level twice but fails to break through, suggesting sellers are defending that area.

A double bottom is the opposite idea: price finds support in a similar low area twice, suggesting buyers are willing to step in there.

These patterns do not guarantee outcomes. They simply help you understand where buyers and sellers have drawn lines in the sand.

Patterns and Options Trades

When you think about taking a call or a put, patterns add another layer of information. A bullish pattern near support might support a call idea. A bearish pattern near resistance might support a put idea.

The key is that patterns are part of a larger process—not the entire process. You still need discipline, direction, risk control, and an exit plan.

A disciplined trader sees a pattern, connects it to the broader story, and then decides whether a trade fits within their rules.
