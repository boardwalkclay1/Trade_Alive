Stage 17 – Entries & Exits with MAs

Moving Averages and Entry Areas

In an uptrend, you might use moving averages to help identify areas where a pullback could offer a more disciplined entry, instead of chasing candles at the very top of a move.

For example, if price has been respecting a rising 20-period moving average, a pullback toward that line—combined with healthy candles and patterns—may offer a more structured place to consider a call.

The point is not to click every time price touches the line. The point is to let the line help you avoid emotional chasing and think in terms of “areas” instead of impulses.

Moving Averages and Exit Ideas

Moving averages can also help with exit thinking. If price has been trending above a moving average and then starts to close below it with strong opposite candles, that may be a sign that the trend is weakening.

Combined with your pre-defined profit target or stop, a change in how price behaves around your moving averages can be a clue to scale out or exit—with discipline, not panic.

In your mock enter/exit simulations, you’ll start to think: “If price loses this moving average and structure, that’s a warning to tighten up or step out.”

Connecting MAs to the Mock Trade Flow

In earlier stages, you practiced entering and exiting trades in a simulated way, choosing direction, contract type, order type, and exit plan.

Now, imagine doing that same simulation but with moving averages on the chart: you choose a direction based on trend, pick entries near pullbacks, and define exits based in part on what happens if price breaks structure around your moving averages.

This is how real structure and real discipline come together: the mock trade flow you’ve already practiced plus moving averages as your guide rails.
